Setting LOW to zero doesn't delete a parent.

M-002 · exact B-side Euler normalization

A finite numerator does not make the subtraction finite.

At large squarefree divisors e ≥ h, there is no positive interior displacement divisible by e. But the unweighted centering term −Nq/e remains part of an infinite absolutely convergent series. The active primes 2 and 3 weight the positive progression, not that negative subtraction.

Positive progression

Weighted by exceptional active primes. Its terms vanish at e ≥ h.

Negative centering

Ordinary Nq/e. The infinite tail survives and enforces exact normalization.

The quick falsifier is q=1, h=2, t=1: prime 2 annihilates the positive part and the literal first-anchor contribution is −1.

M-005 · shifted Suzuki under two physical hypotheses

One exponential-sum theorem, two admissibility arguments.

Both CORE R and LONG-right RL use the same shifted reciprocal-sum engine, but their parent masks and coefficient-variation costs are different. The source's CORE R argument requires a lower logarithmic factor condition with constant 5. In LONG-right RL the honest lower constant is only 4. The source derives the eventual bound again, rather than claiming the stronger hypothesis still holds.

3(2/3 + 1/100) − 3/200 − 2 = 3/200 > 0

In the actual clipped source sum, u = rho + (6qr)t and Y' = Y/v - Y/v'. The progression amplitude is a product of two nonconstant cotangent factors: its endpoint-plus-total-variation norm is at most 2. Both proofs also pay for the two signed main Fourier modes and two signed class modes. The deep fixed-factor estimate needs more than this one lemma, and the two cell populations are not identical.
