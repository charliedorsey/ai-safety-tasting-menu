Logical dependency is not an argument for organizational causation

The surviving Lean sources establish the need for the exact physical partitions, the source-matched analytic suppliers, and the cutoff-uniform bounds within the present proof. They do not establish that a particular tool, routing doctrine, personality, or alternative research lane was causally indispensable to the discovery of that proof. That requires dated process evidence and, for counterfactual claims, evidence we may never have.

The appropriate historical statement is narrower: Charlie describes a deliberate investment in optionality; a contemporaneous research estate contains independent competing routes and reusable interfaces; the winning physical CORE/LONG mathematics later possessed the cutoff flexibility required by the terminal. The record supports the coexistence and sequencing of those facts, and the logical use of the mathematical flexibility. It does not prove that no narrower laboratory could have discovered the same theorem.
