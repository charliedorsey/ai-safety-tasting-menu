The complete mathematical article proves a pointwise transfer for restricted singular-series
coefficients. This guide is an invitation to understand the mathematical
moves before entering the dense analytic proof and its formal source.

\begin{quietbox}
\kicker{The theorem}\vspace{7pt}
\[
 A_{1,q}(a,b;h)-B_{1,q}(a,b;h)=o_q(\log h).
\]
For each \emph{fixed positive squarefree} $q$, the estimate holds along
sufficiently large compatible positive heights, uniformly in compatible
reduced endpoint classes $a,b$. It is a theorem about singular-series
\emph{coefficients}, not actual prime-gap frequencies, growing-$q$ uniformity,
or a theorem at all ranks.
\end{quietbox}

\kicker{The argument in one view}\par\vspace{8pt}
{\Large Two mathematical obligations. One printed statement.}\par

A proof of the desired asymptotic needs two things that cannot replace
one another: the quantities in the theorem must be \emph{exactly} those
estimated by the analytic source, and the difference of those source
quantities must actually be small.

\vspace{6pt}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.48\linewidth}
\begin{quietbox}
\kicker{Road I / representation}\par
\textbf{Identify the Euler quantities.}\par
Convergence and nonvanishing permit primewise division. The two
B boundaries retain their exceptional-prime weights and an infinite
centering subtraction. Reflection changes the anchor correctly.
\[A_{1,q}^{\rm printed}-B_{1,q}^{\rm printed}
  =A_{1,q}^{\rm src}-B_{1,q}^{\rm src}.\]

\kicker{M-001 / exact partition}\par\vspace{8pt}
{\LARGE Where did LOW's parents go?}\par
\subtitle{An empty sector need not mean an omitted contribution.}\par

Every original quarter-parent $e$ has an integer cardinality $|e|$.
For any ordered cutoffs $J_{\rm low}\le J_{\rm high}$, it satisfies
exactly one of the three tests
\[
 |e|<J_{\rm low},\qquad
 J_{\rm low}\le |e|<J_{\rm high},\qquad
 |e|\ge J_{\rm high}.
\]
Because the same signed summand is retained in each branch, the equality
\[
 \mathrm{CENTRAL}=\mathrm{LOW}+\mathrm{MIDDLE}+\mathrm{HIGH}
\]
is established \emph{before} any estimate. The middle sector has another
exact complementary split into CORE and LONG.

\begin{quietbox}
\kicker{The mathematical move}\par
If $|e|=2$, then under symbolic cutoffs $J_{\rm low}=3$ and
$J_{\rm high}=6$ the parent is assigned to LOW. Under
$J_{\rm low}=0$ with the same high cutoff, it is assigned to MIDDLE.
The signed row and all its coefficients are identical. The source's CORE
and LONG estimates allow arbitrary lower cutoffs and therefore cover it.
\end{quietbox}

Setting $J_{\rm low}\equiv0$ makes the predicate $|e|<0$ impossible,
so LOW vanishes \emph{exactly}. This is a reallocation, not a claim that
formerly LOW terms are small on their own. The final source chooses
$\delta=1/4$ for the CORE/LONG geometric split.

\textbf{What would falsify this move?} A partition losing an equality case;
changing the signed row as it changes sectors; or applying a CORE/LONG
bound that is not genuinely uniform in the new cutoff.

\kicker{M-004 / quantitative economy}\par\vspace{8pt}
{\LARGE How does one thousandth survive?}\par
\subtitle{Band governs CORE because a disjoint sum inherits the worst exponent.}\par

After preceding packet tests fail, the Band mask restricts an actual signed
CORE pair to a short logarithmic interval. For source parameters
$\varepsilon_p=(\log h)^{-1/200}$ and $r,s>1$, its complement inequalities
imply
\[
 r^{1/(1+\varepsilon_p)}<s<r^{1+3\varepsilon_p}.
\]
The harmonic interval-mass lemma controls this narrow $s$ range. The
roughness input controls the remaining weighted $B$ factors. These are
\emph{named supplier estimates}, not an argument that individual signed
parents are negligible.

\begin{quietbox}
\kicker{The three payments}\vspace{8pt}
\[
 \underbrace{\frac{1991}{2000}}_{\substack{\text{roughness and}\\\text{interval mass}}}
 +\underbrace{\frac1{400}}_{\text{fixed constants}}
 =\underbrace{\frac{499}{500}}_{\text{physical Band}},
 \]
\[
 \underbrace{\frac{499}{500}}_{\text{worst CORE cell}}
 +\underbrace{\frac1{1000}}_{\text{decoration once}}
 =\boxed{\frac{999}{1000}<1}.
\]
\end{quietbox}

A five-way \emph{sum} is bounded by its largest power, not by the product
of five powers. The whole sector then receives the decoration cost once.
After division by $\log h$, the remaining saving is the positive power
$(\log h)^{-1/1000}$, up to fixed constants.

\textbf{An invalid shortcut:} charging decoration a second time would give
$999/1000+1/1000=1$. That no longer establishes little-$o$ at the
logarithmic scale. The false calculation explains why the real accounting
is part of the proof, not book-keeping trivia.

\kicker{M-003 / geometric eligibility}\par\vspace{8pt}
{\LARGE Why not use Weil at the edge?}\par
\subtitle{A powerful estimate is useful only where its required cofactor exists.}\par

The source's LONG-right routing first excludes reciprocal-packet and
many-factor cases. What remains is split by geometry and the availability
of a prime packet. Put $\eta=(\log h)^{-1/10}$. On EdgeL,
\[
 s^{1/2}\le r<s^{1/2+3\eta}.
\]
This is a thin logarithmic strip of width $3\eta\log s$.
The proof switches summation order and pays for its weighted mass directly:
\[
 \frac1{4000}+\frac1{40}+\frac1{100}+\frac9{10}
 =\frac{3741}{4000}<\frac{19}{20}.
\]
The remaining difference absorbs fixed constants. \emph{No Weil theorem
is used on EdgeL.}

\begin{quietbox}
\kicker{Away from the strip}\par
Outside EdgeL, $r\ge s^{1/2+3\eta}$. If the source's $B$-packet
contains an eligible prime $p\le s^{\eta}$, its extracted cofactor obeys
\[
 \frac rp\ge s^{1/2+2\eta}.
\]
That length is enough for the coupled Weil--Cauchy argument, with
its complete-sum bound
\[
 |K_s(a,b)|\le\tau(s)\sqrt{s\,\gcd(a,b,s)}.
\]
The divisor factor, conductor gcd, Fourier modes and their moments
remain in the estimate. The corresponding off-edge cell is WRL.
\end{quietbox}

An off-edge pair without a valid packet does not enter WRL: it is sent
to ResL. Thus EdgeL and WRL are \emph{disjoint physical populations},
not two competing estimates on an arbitrary pair.

\kicker{M-005 / reciprocal cancellation}\par\vspace{8pt}
{\LARGE Why do R and RL need different proofs?}\par
\subtitle{The same Suzuki engine enters two distinct source geometries.}\par

The analytic supplier controls a shifted reciprocal phase of the form
\[
 S(Y,\vartheta;N,U)=\sum_{N<m\le U}e\!\left(\frac{Y}{m+\vartheta}\right),
 \qquad U\le2N,
\]
with $Y\ge2$, $N\ge1$, $0\le\vartheta\le1$. Its upper bound combines
an exponentially decaying term and a lower-frequency remainder,
\[
 |S|\le C\left(N\exp\!\left[-c\frac{(\log N)^3}{(\log Y)^2}\right]
 +\frac{N^2}{Y}\right).
\]
The actual source clips both $vu$ and $v'u$ and passes to
$u=\rho+Qt$ with $Q=6qr$. Its transformed phase is
$(Y/v-Y/v')/(\rho+Qt)$; the source proves $N\ge1$,
$M\le2N$ and $|Y/v-Y/v'|\ge2Q$. The progressing cotangent
amplitude is nonconstant. Its endpoint-plus-variation norm is
\emph{at most $2$}, proved before Abel summation applies Suzuki.
The four main/class Fourier channels are then handled separately.

\begin{quietbox}
\kicker{Two distinct eligibility tests}\par
CORE's R source proves a lower logarithmic factor condition with
constant \textbf{5}. LONG-right RL can provide only \textbf{4}; it
has a different packet and geometry. The source re-proves the eventual
parameter estimate in the latter range, using
\[
 3\sigma_0-\frac3{200}-2
 =3\left(\frac23+\frac1{100}\right)-\frac3{200}-2
 =\frac3{200}>0.
\]
The positive exponent survives the change in the numerical coefficient,
but not by reusing a hypothesis stronger than the physical source provides.
\end{quietbox}

\textbf{What would invalidate the transfer?} An absent packet; the
wrong residue shift or sign; missing Abel variation; or silently using
CORE's $5$-condition in the LONG $4$-region.

\kicker{M-002 / exact normalization}\par\vspace{8pt}
{\LARGE Why must B retain an infinite tail?}\par
\subtitle{A finite positive count does not permit an infinite subtraction to disappear.}\par

A boundary coefficient first becomes an exact centered divisor series.
With its appropriate first-anchor residue $r$, the printed identity has
the form
\[
 S_1(q,r;h)=C_{f,q}\sum_{e\ge1}\eta_q(e)
 \left(R_e^w(q,r;h)-\frac{N_q(r;h)}e\right).
\]
The positive progression mass $R_e^w$ carries the \emph{active} primes
2 and 3 as local weights. The negative subtraction remains the
\emph{ordinary admissible count} $N_q(r;h)/e$.

\begin{quietbox}
\kicker{The exact move}\par
When $e\ge h$, no positive interior displacement $0<t<h$ is divisible
by $e$, so $R_e^w(q,r;h)=0$. But
\[
 -C_{f,q}N_q(r;h)\sum_{e\ge h}\frac{\eta_q(e)}e
\]
is generally not zero. Absolute convergence justifies the complete
centered series; it does not justify dropping the remaining tail.
The reflected second boundary has its own anchor
$r_b\equiv-b\pmod q$ and must be transported separately.
\end{quietbox}

The small exact falsifier is $q=1,h=2$: the active prime 2 kills the
positive one-point progression and the first-boundary contribution
is $-1$. Any formula that silently sets all active low-prime weights
to one predicts the wrong value.

\textbf{What would break the identity?} An unweighted positive
progression, a weighted centering term, truncated negative tails, or
reflection without the correct endpoint residue.

\kicker{Proof-type versus evidence standing}\par
\textbf{Exact identity}: a literal equality of source populations, Euler
objects, or decompositions; it does not by itself establish cancellation.
\textbf{Estimate}: a bound with physical source masks, hypotheses,
constants and ranges; deep supplier proofs reside in the sealed formal
source unless independently derived on the page.
\textbf{Representation transport}: a checked correspondence between
literal manuscript and source objects, including signs and local factors.
\textbf{Verification standing}: an independent question. Source inspection,
terminal referee acceptance against cached imports, and a full clean
replay are different kinds of evidence.
